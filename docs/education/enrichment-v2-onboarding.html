What AI enrichment
does to our lake.
Read this first.

Our lake is a strict three-layer pipeline: bronze holds everything raw (scraped posts and verbatim AI responses), silver holds cleaned posts and the six conformed AI tables, and gold holds the four analytic marts that answer the owner's questions. Enrichment is slow, paid, and external, so it does not run like a normal data transform. It rides an async seam: submit, wait, harvest. This page teaches the whole design in pictures.

Audience: junior engineers, no Dagster experience required · every term is defined inline or in the glossary

The answer up front

Six AI-conformed tables sit in silver. Three outside AI passes produce them: a vision model reads images and video frames, a transcription model reads audio, a text model reads captions and transcripts. Each pass is slow, paid, and non-deterministic, so it runs through one async seam with one shared job ledger instead of a normal pipeline step. Every raw response lands verbatim in bronze_enrichment_raw first; silver's deterministic conform step turns it into the tables below.

The three passes and their tables:

  • visual pass qwen vision reads the media. One submit's response is mapped into silver_visual_annotations AND silver_visual_summaries.
  • audio pass whisper (runs locally) transcribes reel audio into silver_audio_transcripts.
  • text pass a text LLM reads caption plus transcript. One submit fills silver_text_annotations AND silver_text_summaries. A second, separate submit fills silver_content_classification.

Gold then joins all of it into four marts that answer the owner's three questions. The rest of this page earns that summary: first the lake it lives in, then each piece in order.

The lake, in one picture

Our warehouse is layered. Each layer cleans or adds to what the previous layer produced, and each layer is just a set of tables.

We use the medallion pattern: three layers of tables, each named for a medal, plus a serving layer that dashboards read. The words are the industry's; the idea is simple. Raw in one end, readable answers out the other.

Why three layers? Separation of concerns. Scraping mistakes are fixed in bronze without touching analysis. Cleaning and conforming rules change in silver without re-scraping or re-calling the paid model, because the verbatim responses stay in bronze_enrichment_raw. Gold stays analytic, so marts can be reshaped for new questions without touching either layer below.

What enrichment is, and why it refuses to be a normal step

Enrichment means an outside AI service looks at a post and writes down what it sees and hears, in structured columns.

Concretely: for each post, an AI pass reads the media (images, video frames, audio) and the caption, and answers structured questions. Is a face present? Which brand logos appear? What does the person claim out loud? What kind of hook starts the caption? The raw answers land verbatim in bronze_enrichment_raw, and silver's conform step turns them into columns in the six silver tables. This is different from silver's post cleaning: that applies fixed rules to every row identically. Enrichment produces judgments, and the same post can yield a different answer next week.

Three facts force a different architecture than a normal transform:

  • Slow. A full-corpus enrichment pass takes hours. A normal transform takes seconds to minutes.
  • Paid and rate-limited. Every post is a billable API call to an external provider. You cannot just "re-run the job" carelessly.
  • Non-deterministic. The same input can produce different output. There is no "expected value" to diff against, so provenance (which model, which prompt version, when) has to be recorded next to every answer.
The restaurant analogy

Think of ordering at a restaurant instead of cooking at home. Submit: you place the order at the counter; it takes seconds. Poll: the kitchen cooks; it takes much longer, and you do not stand at the pass watching. You check whether your number is up. Harvest: when it is, you pick up the plate and put it on your table. The restaurant (the AI provider) does the slow work in its own kitchen. Our pipeline places orders and picks up plates; it never stands at the pass blocking everyone else.

A pipeline that modeled enrichment as a normal step would freeze for hours on every run. Dagster (our orchestration tool: it tracks which tables depend on which, and runs steps in the right order) must never wait. The seam below is how we keep the hours outside the pipeline while keeping the results inside the graph.

The six silver tables

Each AI pass's output is conformed into its own silver table. Every table is keyed by post_id and platform, and carries the same provenance envelope.

Read each card as: the table name, what artifact it holds, the columns, and who produces it. The visual pass and the text pass each fill two tables from one AI call; the "same AI call" notes mark those pairs. Validation (parse-ability, required fields, enum conformance, length bounds, cross-field checks) lives in this silver conform step: unparseable or invalid output is quarantined loudly, never a silent NULL and never a dropped row.

The metadata envelope: provenance on every row

Every silver enrichment table carries the same envelope of metadata columns next to its body columns: provider, model, prompt_hash, schema_version, input_modality, content_mime_type, sampling_params_json, run_id, analysed_at.

Because AI output is non-deterministic, the envelope is how we answer "which model, prompted how, on which run, produced this?" later. One honest exception: prompt_hash is NULL on silver_audio_transcripts, because transcription has no prompt; whisper converts speech to text, it is not instructed with a prompt.

Each pass owns its own table, so one pass's provenance can never be overwritten by another pass writing the same row. That defect is exactly what killed the previous single-table facet store.

Two constant keys. Every silver enrichment table is keyed by post_id and platform. That pair is how a silver row joins back to its silver_ig_posts row, and it matches the repo's existing profiles.platform convention. The word domain is reserved for the content niche (what the owner calls "X domain", like dev/AI); it is a body column of silver_content_classification, never a key. That collision (a domain column meaning "instagram" in one table and "dev/AI" in another) is exactly the naming bug this model fixes.

Who waits for whom: the dependency DAG

A DAG (directed acyclic graph) is just a drawing of "this cannot start until that is done." Arrows point from inputs to outputs.

The media itself (images, video frames, audio) feeds the three per-channel passes. The text passes cannot run until the transcript exists, because they read it. Classification runs last because it reads the most.

Possible future change: classification grounding

Classification reads the caption, the transcript, and the visual summaries today. It could also read silver_text_summaries.transcript_summary (the "what is said" condensation) to help ground the labelling. Deferred, not a current dependency — the raw transcript is already an input.

The async seam: submit, poll, harvest

The seam is not a service and not a component — it is the boundary where our pipeline hands work to an outside system and takes results back. Nothing runs "in" the seam. Its contract is two things: one shared external_jobs ledger (a table both sides read and write) and three verbs. Every pass, whatever its engine, rides the same three-step lifecycle over that one ledger.

Submit writes one job into the shared external_jobs ledger. It is sub-second, just a database insert plus an HTTP call; it never does AI work itself. Poll-to-terminal (poll for short) asks the provider whether the job reached a terminal state (done, failed, or empty). Retrieve fetches the finished results. Those three are the seam verbs, and they are the whole contract. Harvest is Dagster's step that composes them: poll to terminal, retrieve, then land the verbatim response in bronze_enrichment_raw, append-only and idempotent on its natural key (re-running writes the same rows again, never duplicates). Silver's conform step then maps them into the six tables. Failures are loud: a bad item is surfaced and re-discoverable, never silently marked done.

Vocabulary: three seam verbs, two Dagster steps

The seam verbs are submit, poll-to-terminal, and retrieve — the contract, and the whole of it. Dagster's steps are submit (which calls that verb) and harvest (which runs the other two verbs in sequence and lands the bytes). So poll in this page is shorthand for poll-to-terminal, and harvest is always a Dagster step — never a verb you call on the seam.

The lifecycle is the same, whatever the scheduler. Executors are pluggable: qwen-vision, whisper, and text-LLM all ride this same submit-poll-harvest pattern over the one shared ledger. Whether a run is triggered by the CLI or by a Dagster sensor, the lifecycle is identical. A provider swap is a metadata change; the seam does not move.

The seam, end to end

The lifecycle above is only half the story. This is the full boundary: who talks to whom, and who owns what.

Read the diagram as one boundary, not four tiers. The seam is the dashed line: a boundary and a contract, with nothing running inside it. Dagster (our orchestrator) sits inside the boundary and owns discovery, batching, the sub-second submit, and the idempotent bronze harvest — orchestration, lineage, quality — and it never calls a provider directly. The only thing that crosses the boundary is the contract: the shared external_jobs ledger (a table both sides read and write) plus the three verbs submit, poll-to-terminal, and retrieve. Everything to the right of the line — the services and the providers — is outside our orchestrator. Harvested rows are keyed by (post_id, platform, workload, prompt_hash, run_id), so re-running the same job re-lands the same rows instead of duplicating them.

Separation of concerns, in one line each. Dagster owns orchestration, lineage, and quality, and never calls a provider directly. The external services own durability, retry, backoff, dead-letter, and media handling. The ledger is the contract between them: both sides read and write it, and nothing else crosses the boundary.

One submit can fan out to many tables

The cost rule that shapes the whole graph: one AI request can return more than one table's worth of output, so one submit's response can map to any number of silver tables.

The rule is general: whatever a single AI response contains, its harvest lands verbatim in bronze_enrichment_raw, and silver's conform step maps it into every table that response serves — two tables, three, or one. Today's examples: the qwen vision call returns annotations AND summaries in a single response, so its conform maps to both visual tables. The text LLM call returns text annotations AND the transcript summary in a single response, so its conform maps to both text tables. Drawing one submit per table instead would send the same media to the provider twice and double the bill. Classification is its own separate submit, because it consumes extra inputs.

Why this matters to the bill. Every submit is a paid API call. If the graph drew one submit per table, the visual pass would run twice and the text pass would run twice, doubling cost for identical answers. One-submit-fans-out is not an optimization we hope to do later; it is the shape of the graph.

Gold: four marts, three questions

The six silver tables are still per-channel pieces. v_post_detail joins them with the silver posts to form the serving base; the canonical metric views are built on that base; and gold composes those views into analytic marts. The whole warehouse exists to answer the owner's three questions about which creators perform well and why, so each mart maps to one of them.

The dashboard's metrics are preserved. Every surface the dashboards use keeps its canonical definition, still built on v_post_detail: hot posts (v_recent_hot_posts), standout and outlier posts (v_outlier_posts, v_engagement_outliers), rising creators and momentum (v_rising_creators, v_creator_profile), post detail (v_post_detail), creator detail (v_creator_profile, v_creator_metrics, v_creator_quality, v_creator_topics), and underperformers (v_underperformer_posts). These views stay canonical and upstream of the marts; the marts only join them, and none is dropped, renamed, or restated.

gold_post_enrichment mart 0: the wide per-post shape

all channel outputs joined on (post_id, platform) · engagement metrics · provenance envelope · composed from the canonical views over v_post_detail

shape of: one post, everything we know about it

gold_creator_performance mart for Q1: who is performing well in X domain?

keyed by (creator_id, platform): follower_count · follower_tier · post_count · median_engagement_score · avg_engagement_score · standout_rate · momentum_ratio · is_rising · dominant_domain

composes v_creator_profile (momentum_ratio, is_rising, avg_engagement_score, dominant_domain) and v_post_follower_context (follower_tier) · defines no metric logic itself

answers: who performs, filterable by domain

gold_content_shape_performance mart for Q2: what shape of content performs in X domain / X topic / for X follower count?

LONG table keyed by (domain, topic, follower_tier, facet_name, facet_value): n_posts · avg_engagement_z · standout_rate · lift_vs_slice_baseline

groups the facets by follower_tier taken from v_post_follower_context, measured with v_post_metrics (engagement z, standout) · re-derives no metric

answers: which facets lift engagement, and by how much

gold_top_posts mart for Q3: what posts do well across all domains, and what is their shape?

keyed by (post_id, platform): rank + percentile across all domains · joined to the full shape + summary/transcript for qualitative reading

answers: the leaderboard, with the why attached

One definition per metric. The serving views own the metric logic: v_post_follower_context defines follower_tier, v_creator_profile defines momentum_ratio, is_rising, avg_engagement_score, and dominant_domain, and v_post_metrics defines engagement z and standout. The gold marts only join and compose these views with the enrichment facets; they never restate the tier buckets or the momentum constants, so a metric changes in exactly one place.

Why "shape" is a long table. Facet schemas evolve: next month's model pass may add a new facet. Storing one row per (facet_name, facet_value) means a new facet is new rows, not a new column, so the mart survives schema evolution without a migration.

Why the map lives in silver, and gold joins

Enrichment output can look like an ordinary transform, so which layer owns it? The rule is the determinism boundary: whatever is a pure function of raw data belongs in silver; whatever joins and aggregates belongs in gold.

  • The raw-to-conformed map is silver's job. ADR-0003 keeps silver hermetic and deterministic: no LLM or API calls in the transform layer. Mapping a verbatim response onto columns is a pure, replayable function of bronze — same input, same output, free to re-run. That is exactly what silver is for.
  • The verbatim response is bronze's job. ADR-0001 names enrichment output "an ingested source": a durable, versioned dataset observed from outside. Its as-observed landing is bronze, so bronze_enrichment_raw stores the verbatim response, immutable and append-only. A schema or mapping change re-derives the silver columns without re-calling the paid model.
  • Gold is the join and aggregate layer. Facets, transcripts, and classification per channel are conformed facts. The owner's three questions are answered by joining and aggregating them — the wide per-post shape, creator rollups, facet lift, and top-post ranks. That analytic work is gold's whole job, and reshaping a mart never touches silver or bronze.
What changed in the re-layer

The previous design put the six AI tables directly in gold, with the raw-response capture recorded as an open decision and the mapping implicit in the harvest. The re-layered design settles it: the harvest lands verbatim responses in bronze_enrichment_raw, the raw-to-conformed map runs deterministically in silver (validation included), and gold keeps only the four analytic marts. The raw-then-conform-then-aggregate chain is now the model, not a pending recommendation.

Naming and provenance rules

Three rules keep the table names stable no matter which AI vendor does the work.

The key is platform, not domain. Every silver enrichment table is keyed by (post_id, platform), matching the repo's existing profiles.platform. domain means the content niche ("dev/AI") and is a body column of silver_content_classification. The old design used domain for both meanings at once; that collision is a hard bug this model removes.
Provider never appears in the name. Vision happens to run on qwen and audio on whisper, but the names stay silver_visual_* and silver_audio_*. A provider swap is a metadata change, never a migration. The table keeps its name; the envelope records who produced each row.
Channel is visual | text | audio, not "video". The visual pass reads single images, carousels, and video frames alike. Naming it "video" would exclude the majority of posts.
No _bound columns. Enum definitions live in a versioned schema registry, referenced by schema_version on every row. We do not sprinkle per-row conformance flags into the tables.
Body columns match schema keys 1:1. The JSON the model returns maps to columns trivially. No renaming layer between the schema and the table.

Two content forms, one explicit rule for missing audio

A post is either a reel/video (with audio) or an image post (without). The passes each post gets follow from that alone.

Reels and video

  • visual annotations + summaries (video frames)
  • audio transcript, status done or empty_audio
  • text annotations + summary (caption + transcript)
  • text classification, after those land

Images and carousels

  • visual annotations + summaries, including per-image image_summaries_json
  • audio a row still exists: transcript_status = no_audio_source
  • text annotations + summary (caption only, no transcript)
  • text classification

No silent NULLs, no missing rows. An image post has no audio, but its silver_audio_transcripts row still exists with the explicit status no_audio_source. A query never has to guess whether NULL means "no audio" or "transcription failed or has not run yet". The same status column separates pending, done, and empty_audio (audio existed but contained no speech).

Glossary

Every term this page uses, defined once, in plain words.

asset
A named, persistent thing our code produces, usually a table. Dagster tracks each asset and how assets depend on each other.
ASR
Automatic speech recognition: turning spoken audio into text. Whisper is our ASR engine. Also called speech-to-text (STT).
async
Work that starts and finishes at different times, so the caller does not wait for it. Our enrichment submits a job and harvests results later.
bronze_enrichment_raw
The bronze landing zone for every raw AI response, whatever workload produced it (vision, transcription, text LLM). Verbatim, immutable, append-only, idempotent on its natural key. Because it keeps what the model actually said, a schema or mapping change re-derives silver without re-calling the paid model.
bronze / silver / gold
The medallion layers: bronze = raw as-observed (scraped posts and verbatim AI responses), silver = cleaned posts and conformed enrichment tables, gold = the four analytic marts. Serving views sit on top for dashboards.
DAG
Directed acyclic graph: a drawing of dependencies where arrows mean "needs first". Acyclic means no circular waits.
Dagster
The orchestrator. It knows the asset graph, runs steps in dependency order, and triggers work on schedules or external events. It never does the AI work itself.
facet
One structured observation the AI records about a post, like face_present or hook_type. A table of facets is a table of answers to fixed questions.
fan-out
One input producing several outputs. Here: one paid AI request's response maps to one or more silver tables (today two each for the visual and text passes).
gold marts
The four analytic tables gold holds: gold_post_enrichment (the wide per-post shape), gold_creator_performance, gold_content_shape_performance, and gold_top_posts. Each of the latter three answers one of the owner's three questions.
harvest
Dagster's step — not a seam verb. It polls to terminal, retrieves the finished results, and lands them verbatim in bronze_enrichment_raw, idempotently: it composes the seam verbs poll-to-terminal and retrieve.
idempotent upsert
Writing that is safe to repeat: re-running the harvest for the same job produces the same rows, never duplicates.
ledger
The shared external_jobs table: the single contract between Dagster and the outside services. Dagster writes submits; the services drain jobs and report status; nothing else crosses the boundary.
materialization
Running the code that produces (or refreshes) an asset. "Materialize the table" means "recompute it now".
medallion
The bronze-to-silver-to-gold layering pattern for lakes. Quality and meaning are added progressively, layer by layer.
poll-to-terminal
One of the three seam verbs: asking whether a job has reached a terminal state (done, failed, or empty). poll is shorthand for it.
provenance
The recorded origin of a value: which provider, which model, which prompt version, which run, when. Stored in the metadata envelope.
prompt_hash
A fingerprint of the exact prompt version used. NULL on transcripts, because transcription involves no prompt.
qwen-batch-service
Our own external service (FastAPI + its own SQLite job store) that drains enrichment jobs against qwen via OpenRouter and does ffmpeg frame-sampling for reels. Separate process, separate durable state.
seam
The boundary where our pipeline hands work to an outside service and takes results back. Its whole contract is three verbs: submit, poll-to-terminal, and retrieve.
serving views
Thin, read-only SQL views (like v_post_metrics) shaped for dashboards rather than storage. They are built on the serving base v_post_detail and stay upstream of the gold marts, which compose them.
submit
Both a seam verb and Dagster's step that calls it: sub-second, it records an enrichment job in the shared ledger and sends it to the provider. No AI work happens in the submit.
terminal status
A job state that will not change again: done, failed, or empty. Polling stops once a job is terminal.
