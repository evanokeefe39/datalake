What AI enrichment
does to our lake.
Read this first.

Our lake is a three-layer pipeline: bronze holds raw scraped posts, silver holds cleaned ones, and gold is where AI adds meaning: what the post shows, says, and is about. Enrichment is slow, paid, and external, so it does not run like a normal data transform. It rides an async seam: submit, wait, harvest. This page teaches the whole design in pictures.

Audience: junior engineers, no Dagster experience required · every term is defined inline or in the glossary

The answer up front

Six AI-written tables sit in gold. Three outside AI passes produce them: a vision model reads images and video frames, a transcription model reads audio, a text model reads captions and transcripts. Each pass is slow, paid, and non-deterministic, so it runs through one async seam with one shared job ledger instead of a normal pipeline step.

The three passes and their tables:

  • visual pass qwen vision reads the media. One submit fills gold_visual_annotations AND gold_visual_summaries.
  • audio pass whisper (runs locally) transcribes reel audio into gold_audio_transcripts.
  • text pass a text LLM reads caption plus transcript. One submit fills gold_text_annotations AND gold_text_summaries. A second, separate submit fills gold_content_classification.

The rest of this page earns that summary: first the lake it lives in, then each piece in order.

The lake, in one picture

Our warehouse is layered. Each layer cleans or adds to what the previous layer produced, and each layer is just a set of tables.

We use the medallion pattern: three layers of tables, each named for a medal, plus a serving layer that dashboards read. The words are the industry's; the idea is simple. Raw in one end, readable answers out the other.

Why three layers? Separation of concerns. Scraping mistakes are fixed in bronze without touching analysis. Cleaning rules change in silver without re-scraping. AI enrichment lives in gold so it can be re-run, audited, and paid for separately from everything else.

What enrichment is, and why it refuses to be a normal step

Enrichment means an outside AI service looks at a post and writes down what it sees and hears, in structured columns.

Concretely: for each post, an AI pass reads the media (images, video frames, audio) and the caption, and answers structured questions. Is a face present? Which brand logos appear? What does the person claim out loud? What kind of hook starts the caption? The answers land as columns in gold tables. This is different from silver's cleaning: silver applies fixed rules to every row identically. Enrichment produces judgments, and the same post can yield a different answer next week.

Three facts force a different architecture than a normal transform:

  • Slow. A full-corpus enrichment pass takes hours. A normal transform takes seconds to minutes.
  • Paid and rate-limited. Every post is a billable API call to an external provider. You cannot just "re-run the job" carelessly.
  • Non-deterministic. The same input can produce different output. There is no "expected value" to diff against, so provenance (which model, which prompt version, when) has to be recorded next to every answer.
The restaurant analogy

Think of ordering at a restaurant instead of cooking at home. Submit: you place the order at the counter; it takes seconds. Poll: the kitchen cooks; it takes much longer, and you do not stand at the pass watching. You check whether your number is up. Harvest: when it is, you pick up the plate and put it on your table. The restaurant (the AI provider) does the slow work in its own kitchen. Our pipeline places orders and picks up plates; it never stands at the pass blocking everyone else.

A pipeline that modeled enrichment as a normal step would freeze for hours on every run. Dagster (our orchestration tool: it tracks which tables depend on which, and runs steps in the right order) must never wait. The seam below is how we keep the hours outside the pipeline while keeping the results inside the graph.

The six gold tables

Each AI pass writes its own table. Every table is keyed by post_id and domain, and carries the same provenance envelope.

Read each card as: the table name, what artifact it holds, the columns, and who produces it. The visual pass and the text pass each fill two tables from one AI call; the "same AI call" notes mark those pairs.

The metadata envelope: provenance on every row

Every enrichment table carries the same envelope of metadata columns next to its body columns: provider, model, prompt_hash, schema_version, input_modality, content_mime_type, sampling_params_json, run_id, analysed_at.

Because AI output is non-deterministic, the envelope is how we answer "which model, prompted how, on which run, produced this?" later. One honest exception: prompt_hash is NULL on gold_audio_transcripts, because transcription has no prompt; whisper converts speech to text, it is not instructed with a prompt.

Each pass owns its own table, so one pass's provenance can never be overwritten by another pass writing the same row. That defect is exactly what killed the previous single-table facet store.

Two constant keys. Every enrichment table is keyed by post_id and domain. That pair is how a gold row joins back to its silver post, and how the same post can be enriched independently per domain context.

Who waits for whom: the dependency DAG

A DAG (directed acyclic graph) is just a drawing of "this cannot start until that is done." Arrows point from inputs to outputs.

The media itself (images, video frames, audio) feeds the three per-channel passes. The text passes cannot run until the transcript exists, because they read it. Classification runs last because it reads the most.

The async seam: submit, poll, harvest

One shared job ledger sits between our pipeline and the AI providers. Every pass, whatever its engine, rides the same three-step lifecycle.

Submit writes one job into the shared external_jobs ledger. It is sub-second, just a database insert plus an HTTP call; it never does AI work itself. Poll: a background check asks the provider whether the job reached a terminal state (done, failed, or empty). Harvest: when a job is done, we retrieve the results and write them into gold with an idempotent upsert (re-running writes the same rows again, never duplicates). Failures are loud: a bad item is surfaced and re-discoverable, never silently marked done.

The seam is the lifecycle, not the scheduler. Executors are pluggable: qwen-vision, whisper, and text-LLM all ride this same submit-poll-harvest pattern over the one shared ledger. Whether a run is triggered by the CLI or by a Dagster sensor, the lifecycle is identical. A provider swap is a metadata change; the seam does not move.

One submit fans out to two tables

The cost rule that shapes the whole graph: one AI request returns more than one table's worth of output.

The qwen vision call returns annotations AND summaries in a single response. The text LLM call returns text annotations AND the transcript summary in a single response. Drawing one submit per table would send the same media to the provider twice and double the bill. So the graph has one visual submit whose harvest fans out to both visual tables, and one text submit whose harvest fans out to both text tables. Classification is its own submit, because it consumes extra inputs.

Why this matters to the bill. Every submit is a paid API call. If the graph drew one submit per table, the visual pass would run twice and the text pass would run twice, doubling cost for identical answers. One-submit-fans-out is not an optimization we hope to do later; it is the shape of the graph.

Naming and provenance rules

Three rules keep the table names stable no matter which AI vendor does the work.

Provider never appears in the name. Vision happens to run on qwen and audio on whisper, but the names stay gold_visual_* and gold_audio_*. A provider swap is a metadata change, never a migration. The table keeps its name; the envelope records who produced each row.
Channel is visual | text | audio, not "video". The visual pass reads single images, carousels, and video frames alike. Naming it "video" would exclude the majority of posts.
No _bound columns. Enum definitions live in a versioned schema registry, referenced by schema_version on every row. We do not sprinkle per-row conformance flags into the tables.
Body columns match schema keys 1:1. The JSON the model returns maps to columns trivially. No renaming layer between the schema and the table.

Two content forms, one explicit rule for missing audio

A post is either a reel/video (with audio) or an image post (without). The passes each post gets follow from that alone.

Reels and video

  • visual annotations + summaries (video frames)
  • audio transcript, status done or empty_audio
  • text annotations + summary (caption + transcript)
  • text classification, after those land

Images and carousels

  • visual annotations + summaries, including per-image image_summaries_json
  • audio a row still exists: transcript_status = no_audio_source
  • text annotations + summary (caption only, no transcript)
  • text classification

No silent NULLs, no missing rows. An image post has no audio, but its gold_audio_transcripts row still exists with the explicit status no_audio_source. A query never has to guess whether NULL means "no audio" or "transcription failed or has not run yet". The same status column separates pending, done, and empty_audio (audio existed but contained no speech).

Glossary

Every term this page uses, defined once, in plain words.

asset
A named, persistent thing our code produces, usually a table. Dagster tracks each asset and how assets depend on each other.
ASR
Automatic speech recognition: turning spoken audio into text. Whisper is our ASR engine. Also called speech-to-text (STT).
async
Work that starts and finishes at different times, so the caller does not wait for it. Our enrichment submits a job and harvests results later.
bronze / silver / gold
The medallion layers: bronze = raw as-scraped, silver = cleaned, gold = enriched with meaning. Serving views sit on top for dashboards.
DAG
Directed acyclic graph: a drawing of dependencies where arrows mean "needs first". Acyclic means no circular waits.
Dagster
The orchestrator. It knows the asset graph, runs steps in dependency order, and triggers work on schedules or external events. It never does the AI work itself.
facet
One structured observation the AI records about a post, like face_present or hook_type. A table of facets is a table of answers to fixed questions.
fan-out
One input producing several outputs. Here: one paid AI request fills two gold tables at harvest.
harvest
The step that retrieves finished results from the provider and writes them into gold with an idempotent upsert.
idempotent upsert
Writing that is safe to repeat: re-running the harvest for the same job produces the same rows, never duplicates.
materialization
Running the code that produces (or refreshes) an asset. "Materialize the table" means "recompute it now".
medallion
The bronze-to-silver-to-gold layering pattern for lakes. Quality and meaning are added progressively, layer by layer.
provenance
The recorded origin of a value: which provider, which model, which prompt version, which run, when. Stored in the metadata envelope.
prompt_hash
A fingerprint of the exact prompt version used. NULL on transcripts, because transcription involves no prompt.
seam
The boundary where our pipeline hands work to an outside service and takes results back: submit, poll, harvest.
serving views
Thin, read-only SQL views (like v_post_metrics) built on top of gold, shaped for dashboards rather than for storage.
submit
The sub-second step that records an enrichment job in the shared ledger and sends it to the provider. No AI work happens in the submit.
terminal status
A job state that will not change again: done, failed, or empty. Polling stops once a job is terminal.
