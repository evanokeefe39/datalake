# Adversarial / Red-Team Review of the Bin-then-Readmit Proposal

Panel seat: ADVERSARY (round 2) · 2026-09-14 · Read-only; this file is the deliverable.
Target: [`../seam-readmission-brief.md`](../seam-readmission-brief.md).
Stance: find where the proposal is WRONG, not where it is incomplete. Where an attack fails, that is said too — a panel that only finds faults is as useless as one that finds none.

**One-line verdict up front:** the proposal's *deletion* half is honest arithmetic and survives attack, but its *re-admission* half is a promissory note backed by no scheduled work anywhere in the plan: "temporarily" is load-bearing and unscheduled, the interface it appeals to has zero production callers and one known per-call-parameter defect, and the saved double work is partly relocated, not saved. The correct reading is: bin Gemini and SAY it might be permanent, or name the trigger and the owner now.

## 1. Q6 first: is "temporarily" honest? The run_lifecycle precedent, tested

The proposal's word is "temporarily" (brief:3). This is the claim most worth breaking, because the repo itself supplies the falsification precedent the proposal must overcome.

**The attack: "temporarily" is doing rhetorical work that no scheduled work supports.**

1. **The re-admission appears in no plan unit.** A grep of `remediation-plan.md` for `re-admi|bring.*back|return.*Gemini|readmi` returns matches ONLY inside the brief itself — the plan's units are W0, W-FREEZE, W1…W9, and none of them contains a Gemini re-admission task, a trigger condition, or a decision gate for it (`remediation-plan.md` — zero hits; checked 2026-09-14). The closest the plan comes is W5's CI-grep pair asserting `build_adapter("gemini")` "constructs while no Dagster entry point references Gemini" — *unused but available* (plan:515). That is a convention asserting a capability, not a plan exercising one. The word "temporarily" lives only in the brief; the plan it rides on contains only the binning half. **A temporary measure whose return has no scheduled trigger, no owner, and no unit is a permanent retirement with a fig leaf, by definition of what makes work temporary.**
2. **The seam precedent is exactly the pattern the proposal repeats.** `seam.run_lifecycle` (seam.py:145) has zero production callers in `src/` — the only occurrences of the name are the definition and its unit tests (grep over `src; tests`: seam.py:145 def; test_seam.py:109-139 fakes only). The interface's own provenance is a spike (`enrichment-spike/spike_defs/adapter.py:381` `run_lifecycle`, same signature) that was **never wired into this repo** — the proposal now cites an interface that has never driven a production item as the thing that will carry the re-admission. This is the specific, checkable optimism named in the seat focus: *a re-admission story routed through a seam whose lifecycle has never been the execution path of anything*. Every previous claim in this repo that "the seam exists, providers can come back through it" has coexisted with 9 of 11 call sites bypassing it (brief:51-53) — the seam's history is bypass, not admission.
3. **The freeze removes the growth source that would force a decision.** W-FREEZE retires `analysis.py:179`'s `INSERT INTO gold_analyses` (plan:434-435) and freezes the corpus; W6/W7 then migrate and prove parity against a static baseline. After W9, the system has no scheduled pressure that requires a second provider: qwen-through-the-service is the only live transport, the fork decision (plan:529, after W1) is already human-gated, and the plan's own dependency graph contains no node downstream of W9. Post-W9, re-admission is nobody's backlog item. Interval length is therefore unbounded — and an unbounded interval converts "temporarily" into "permanently, with an escape hatch we never pull."

**The counter-case, argued seriously (where the attack fails):**

- **The capability survives as tested code, not as convention.** Unlike the zombie-queue pattern the post-mortem round 1 already convicted, the Gemini capability after W-FREEZE/W5 is not green tests resting on deleted code: `DirectBatchAdapter` is registered in `ADAPTER_REGISTRY` (adapters.py:359-360), its round-trip, state-normalization, and terminal-agreement behavior is under real unit tests (test_adapters.py:194-232, :301-310), and `build_adapter("direct_batch")` resolves (test_adapters.py:286-293). The deletion removes *entry points and call sites*, not the provider seam. That is materially different from dead weight: the re-admission cost is an entry-point rewrite, not a provider rewrite.
- **A plausible named trigger exists, even if the plan doesn't write it down:** the free-tier gate. `DirectBatchAdapter.health()` returns False on FREE tier and True on TIER_1 (adapters.py:349-352; test_adapters.py:238-241), and the service-backed adapter exists precisely because the *synchronous* qwen provider needs a job store (adapters.py:229-231). If the account reaches Tier 1 — a billing/credential event, not an engineering event — the economics of the direct batch path change materially, and that is a trigger an owner could actually notice without reading a panel doc.
- **The "permanence" claim itself is unfalsifiable in its strong form.** I cannot prove a negative about future decisions; the honest form of my attack is the weaker, testable one: *as written, nothing in the artifact set schedules, triggers, or owns re-admission*. That weaker form is sufficient: a proposal that says "temporary" while shipping only the binning half is misdescribing itself regardless of what the owner later intends.

**Adjudication: "temporarily" is NOT established as honest — but it is not proven dishonest either; it is UNSCHEDULED, which is the actionable defect.** The attack lands on the absence of trigger/owner/unit (points 1 and 3 above are mechanical grep facts), not on psychology. The finding is conditional and cheap to fix: the proposal becomes honest if the freeze ships with a written trigger ("Tier 1 granted", or "qwen service unavailable > N hours") and a named decider. Without that, the panel should treat the sentence "we'll bring it back through the seam" as a mood, not a plan. The run_lifecycle precedent is the tell: the repo has already produced one interface that was never wired and one dormant write path that stayed dormant until a panel noticed — in both cases the documents around them claimed capability while the artifact set claimed nothing.

## 2. Q4: what rots in the interval — recoverable vs. not

The interval is unbounded (§1), so rot classification matters more than usual. Split by the criterion: **can a later engineer fix it by rerunning a standard procedure, or does the fix require knowledge that only exists while the path is alive?**

**Recoverable rot (name it, accept the cost):**

| Rot | Evidence it will happen | Cheapest mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| google-genai SDK/API version drift | `gemini_batch.py` is wrapped by a lazy-import adapter (adapters.py:232-235, 277) — so zero production use means zero friction against deprecations until the return | Pin the SDK version in the lockfile; one quarterly `uv lock --upgrade-package google-genai` + rerun `test_adapters.py` round-trip. Cost: minutes per quarter. |
| Credential/tier expiry | `health()` depends on `GeminiTierConfig.detect` (adapters.py:349-352) — the free-tier gate is exactly the thing that goes stale | None needed beyond docs; credential renewal is a standing ops procedure. |
| Environment/entry-point wiring drift | The Dagster entry points are deleted (plan:169, 334-336); the definitions objects, schedules, sensors muscle memory decay | Document the re-wire recipe in the unit's result note (the plan already requires result notes, plan:194). |

**NOT recoverable by procedure — knowledge-rot:**

1. **The fixtures and tests encode the CURRENT envelope shape, and they will assert a shape that no longer exists.** `tests/fixtures/real_envelope_gemini.json` is a *new* artifact W-FREEZE creates (plan:194) from real envelopes captured before the freeze — but the plan's own W2/W3 debt table shows the Gemini entry-point tests (`test_submit_job.py`, `test_harvest_sensor.py`, `test_batch_media_resilience.py`, `test_batch_inline_media.py`, plan:275-278) are slated as green-able *only once the queue is gone*. After W3/W5 execute their deletions, those tests do not rot — they are deleted. On re-admission, the test suite for the returned provider must be **re-authored against whatever the seam looks like at that future date**, by someone who has never seen the old tests. This is rot of the kind that does not announce itself: nothing fails; the knowledge just isn't there. *Cheapest mitigation: the fixture is the right artifact (envelopes outlive code); ALSO freeze the terminal-state mapping tests (`_GEMINI_STATES`, adapters.py:214) as a spec document, not just code — that mapping is the adapter's only provider-specific contract.*
2. **Interface drift away from what the second provider needs (the max_tokens precedent, recurring).** The brief itself documents the failure mode: the seam's submit verb "cannot express a per-call parameter, which the bypass comments cite as the reason for bypassing" (brief:53-55). W5 fixes it for qwen by adding `JobSpec` (plan:337-338) — *the fix is designed by the surviving consumer*. Once Gemini is the absent consumer, every subsequent seam change is made with one provider's requirements in the room. This is not SDK drift (recoverable by upgrade); it is spec drift toward a single-consumer interface, and the drift is silent because the absent provider has no test in CI to go red. *Cheapest mitigation: when W5 lands JobSpec, the review checklist asks explicitly "does DirectBatchAdapter still satisfy this signature without re-introducing the bypass reason?" — one sentence in the W5 result note.*
3. **The handle-encoding divergence rots into a live defect, not a latent one.** Today `submit.py:170` joins with `"|"` and `DirectBatchAdapter` joins with `","` (adapters.py:287) — the brief flags this (brief:58). In a live two-provider world this is caught by contention; in a one-provider world the surviving convention hardens, and the divergence is rediscovered at re-admission time as a *bug in the returning code* rather than a known seam defect. *Mitigation: fold the normalization into W5's C3 (handle encoding) work — it is already a named control of that unit (plan:310).*

**Net:** the recoverable rot is cheap and should be named-and-accepted; the knowledge-rot items 1-2 are the real risk, and both have near-zero-cost mitigations that fit inside units the plan already has (W5's JobSpec review note, W-FREEZE's fixture). The proposal is not wrong to bin; it is wrong to bin *without* these three one-line mitigations attached.

## 3. The sequencing claim: is the saved double work real, or relocated?

The proposal's core economic claim (plan:162-177, 341-346): W-FREEZE converts nine Gemini *migrations* into nine *deletions*, saving W5 ~80%.

**The attack: the work is partly relocated into a harder form, not deleted.**

The nine sites that get deleted are not just call sites — their *tests* are the fixtures of the old path: `test_submit_job.py` (6E+1F), `test_harvest_sensor.py` (8E), `test_harvest_landing.py` (8E), `test_media_upload_op.py` (5E), `test_batch_media_resilience.py` (4E), `test_batch_inline_media.py` (2F) (plan:275-278). W2's own note says the deleted-behavior coverage must be replaced by tests "asserting the retired behavior it [the red file] asserted, so the coverage is not silently lost" (plan:274) — but that obligation applies to the *queue*, and for the Gemini sites it resolves to: coverage deleted with the sites.

Now run the ledger honestly. Under migrate-first (W5 without W-FREEZE), the nine sites cost: nine seam rewires + keeping their tests green against the seam. Under bin-then-readmit, the nine sites cost zero at W5 — and re-admission later costs: two new Dagster entry points wired through `run_lifecycle` + `build_adapter("direct_batch")`, plus **re-authoring the round-trip/terminal/retry test suite for the returned path** (per §2's knowledge-rot item 1), plus reconciling handle encoding and terminal predicates that hardened around the single surviving provider (§2 item 3). The first cost is real and now; the second cost is real, later, and *without the deleted code as a working reference*. The proposal's framing ("deletion instead of adaptation", plan:175) counts the first column and never books the second.

**Where the attack FAILS — and this is the strongest fact on this axis:** the deletion-vs-migration savings are genuine *if the migration target is not the re-admission vehicle*. Migrating the nine sites would have meant teaching the legacy queue's entry points to speak the seam while the legacy queue still exists — work that is then torn out again by W3's queue deletion (plan:253-256 says exactly this about W2's green tests: "re-implementing the queue W3 is removing"). The bin-then-readmit plan does not relocate *that* work at all: it deletes it. The relocated part is only the *test re-authoring*, which in the migrate-first world would also have to be rewritten anyway (those tests are marked 6E/8E — debt against the old envelope, not the seam). **Adjudication: the double-work claim SURVIVES for the production code (the nine-site migration is work genuinely avoided, not moved), and fails only for test coverage, where the work is relocated from "rewritten during W5 with context" to "rewritten cold, later."** The claim should be defended on the first ground only; the plan currently claims both silently.

One more hole in the sequencing, independent of the ledger: the plan says W-FREEZE must precede W1 so the baseline "cannot move mid-migration" (plan:535-538). But the freeze does double duty — corpus freeze AND provider retirement — and only the first is load-bearing for W1/W6/W7. Bundling a semantically optional retirement (usage) into a load-bearing freeze (write path) is how optional parts inherit the load-bearing part's urgency: after W-FREEZE executes, re-admission requires reopening a unit the plan has marked decided (plan:128). The two should be separable events; as written, the re-admission door closes when the corpus freezes, which is not a technical requirement — it is a scheduling accident.

## 4. The interface-adequacy presumption: available for falsification, partially falsified

The proposal's central promise: "bring it back according to the interface / seam we defined" (brief:3-4). This presumes the interface is adequate for a second provider. Test it against the brief's own defect list:

| Seam property | Adequate for Gemini's return? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Canonical state vocabulary | **Yes** — `DirectBatchAdapter` already normalizes the full native state set into canonical states, with unknown-state rejection | adapters.py:210-214; test_adapters.py:210-232; test_adapters.py:301-310 proves terminal-agreement with both adapters |
| Registry / one naming point | **Yes** — registration-by-import, `build_adapter` is the only name lookup | seam.py:119-139; adapters.py:357-360; test_adapters.py:286-298 |
| No-ledger rule (ADR-0013) | **Plausibly yes** — Gemini already tracks its own jobs; the adapter wraps the repo verbs rather than duplicating tracking | adapters.py:228-231 |
| Per-call parameters on submit | **No — known defect, fix scheduled** — `max_tokens` cannot be expressed through the seam verb; fix is `JobSpec` in W5 | brief:53-55; plan:337-338 |
| ONE terminal predicate | **No — known divergence, no scheduled fix** — `gemini_batch.is_terminal` vs the service's state handling; W5 fixes "one terminal predicate" only for the qwen transport (plan:340) | brief:56-57 |
| ONE handle encoding | **No — known divergence, no scheduled fix** — `"|"` (submit.py:170) vs `","` (adapters.py:287) | brief:58 |
| Chunked composite handles | **Yes by accident, untested in anger** — the spike's `ChunkedDirectBatchAdapter` (spike adapter.py:269) was folded into `DirectBatchAdapter`'s comma-joined handle (adapters.py:16-19); the spike's chunked adapter carried per-chunk orchestration logic that this repo's adapter inherits only as "join with commas" and pray | spike adapter.py:266-359 vs adapters.py:276-287 |

**Adjudication: the presumption is ~half true.** The state-vocabulary and registry halves are genuinely provider-neutral — tested, symmetric across both adapters, the strongest part of the seam. But three named defects (per-call params, terminal predicate, handle encoding) are exactly the "designed around one provider's shape, the other only nominally" pattern Q1 asks about — and the proposal's promise dissolves into "bring it back through an interface with three known single-provider distortions, of which one is scheduled for repair, two are not." None of this kills the proposal: W5's `JobSpec` and the W5 controls C2/C3 (plan:310) are the right vehicles for the other two. But the proposal, as worded, claims the interface is ready ("designed for exactly this", brief:25-26) when its own defect list says it is *becoming* adequate, contingent on W5 landing those fixes. The honest wording is "bring it back through the seam **after W5 closes the three named defects**" — without the qualifier, the promise is a forecast wearing a fact's clothes.

## 5. Summary of findings (priority order)

1. **HIGH (proposal integrity):** "Temporarily" is unscheduled. The plan's units W0–W9 contain no re-admission task, trigger, or decision gate (grep: zero hits in `remediation-plan.md`); the freeze bundles provider retirement into the corpus freeze, so re-admission requires reopening a unit marked decided (plan:128, 535-538). *Falsified if: a result note names a trigger (e.g., Tier-1 grant — observable via `health()`, adapters.py:349-352) and an owner before W-FREEZE executes.* The proposal is salvageable with one sentence; as written, the word "temporarily" is the least-supported claim in the document.
2. **HIGH (interface adequacy overstated):** The seam is half-adequate: state vocabulary and registry are proven provider-neutral (test_adapters.py:194-310), but per-call params, terminal predicate, and handle encoding are three known single-provider distortions, one scheduled for repair (JobSpec, plan:337-338), two not. *Falsified if: W5's scope demonstrably closes all three, or the two unscheduled ones are shown not to matter for the direct-batch path.*
3. **MEDIUM (double-work claim half-wrong):** The nine-site migration savings are real (the alternative was rewiring into a queue W3 deletes — plan:253-256); but the test-coverage work is relocated to a cold rewrite at re-admission, and the plan books neither. *Falsified if: W-FREEZE's fixture set (plan:194) plus frozen terminal-mapping tests are judged sufficient to make the rewrite non-cold.*
4. **CONFIRMED (no attack found):** The deletion half is honest arithmetic. `DirectBatchAdapter` survives as registered, tested code (adapters.py:357-360; test_adapters.py:194-232), so binning usage does not destroy the capability — this is categorically different from the zombie-queue failure round 1 convicted, and the W-FREEZE-before-W5 ordering is genuinely correct on the production-code axis.
